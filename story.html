Where Do NHL Goals Come From?

Author

Andrew Lucas

Every game, hundreds of pucks fly toward the net.

But only some of them come from dangerous places.

A data story across 19 NHL seasons.

The Question

Since 2007, over 2 million shot attempts have been fired across the NHL. Some found the back of the net. Most didn’t. The data doesn’t lie — it maps out exactly where offense lives on the ice, and where it dies.

Where do the goals actually come from?

Act I: The Big Picture

Zoom out. Every shot on the ice, from every season, overlaid on a single rink. The bright spots reveal the hot zones — the areas where offense concentrates.

The glow is brightest between the faceoff circles, just above the goal line. This is the slot — the most dangerous real estate in hockey. The closer you are to the net, the more likely you are to score.

But not all players operate in the same neighborhoods.

Act II: The Cast of Characters

Every position has its own shooting identity.

Centers own the slot. They lurk in the high-traffic corridor right in front of the net, reading the play, waiting for the pass, and pouncing on loose pucks. Their heatmap is a concentrated glow right in the heart of the offensive zone.

Defensemen shoot from everywhere — but especially from the point, near the blue line. Their job isn’t always to score. It’s to get pucks through traffic, create rebounds, and keep plays alive. Their shots come from further out, from wider angles, from places where goals are rare but chaos is abundant.

Left Wings and Right Wings work the edges. They patrol the faceoff circles and the half-boards, looking for shooting lanes that the centers and defensemen can’t find. Their footprints are asymmetric — each wing favoring their side of the ice, carving out their own territory.

Every position plays a different game. The ice is not one story — it’s four.

Act III: The Goal Line

Not all shots are created equal. A blast from the blue line and a tap-in from the crease are both “shots” — but they don’t score at the same rate.

The data makes this brutally clear. Shot density spreads across the entire offensive zone — wide, diffuse, hopeful. Goal density collapses into a tight cluster right in front of the net. The gap between where shots come from and where goals happen is the gap between hope and results.

Distance kills scoring chances. From the blue line, hundreds of shots yield almost no goals. From the crease, almost every shot is dangerous. The ice has a geography, and the goalies know it — but the shooters do, too.

The Takeaway

The NHL is a game of angles and inches. This data tells a clear story:

  1. The slot is king. The area between the faceoff circles is where games are won.
  2. Position dictates opportunity. Centers get the best looks. Defensemen create from a distance. Wingers find edges.
  3. Proximity = probability. Every foot closer to the net dramatically increases your chance of scoring.

The ice surface hasn’t changed in a century. But how teams use it — that’s the game within the game.
